TITLE: Spatio-Temporal Changes in Forest Cover and Forest Fragmentation in the Northern Western Ghats
SOURCE: Badhe, Y.P. et al. (2024). International Journal of Ecology and Environmental Sciences, 50(1): 147-155.
REGION: Northern Western Ghats, India — study sites: Anjaneri, Panchgani, Kas Plateau.
STUDY PERIOD: 1989-2015 (26 years).

SUMMARY:
Using the Landscape Fragmentation Tool (100m edge-width threshold), this study classified forest into core (further split by patch size), edge, perforated and patch categories across three Western Ghats sites, tracking change over 26 years.

Findings by site:
- Anjaneri: non-forest area increased 21%, core forest decreased 15%.
- Panchgani: non-forest area increased 29% (most severe), core forest decreased 14%.
- Kas Plateau: non-forest area increased 14%, core forest declined 18% overall, with core forest specifically reduced by 26.37% by 2015.

Across all three sites, the pattern was consistent: land-use conversion (agriculture, monoculture plantation, settlement expansion) steadily converted core, contiguous forest into edge and patch forest — i.e., fragmentation. The authors note that fragmented forest edges become "more vulnerable," reporting increased incidence of invasive species and human-wildlife conflict as a direct consequence of fragmentation.

RELEVANCE: Provides a real, quantified India example of the land-use → habitat fragmentation → species/biodiversity decline chain, with hard percentage figures over a multi-decade period, directly usable for a "monoculture/land-use change" recommendation scenario.
